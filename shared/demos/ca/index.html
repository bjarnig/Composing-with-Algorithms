Cellular Automata

One local rule, applied to every cell at once, over and over. Nothing in the rule describes the pattern that comes out of it. Elementary automata after Wolfram; the numbering is his. Mirrors CA.scd and WolframCA.scd.

Start with the gain low. Dense rules at a high rate are loud and bright.

Every cell looks at itself and its two neighbours, three cells, so eight possible neighbourhoods. A rule is one choice of new state for each of the eight, which is eight bits, which is why there are exactly 256 elementary rules. The eight boxes above the diagram are that rule, drawn out. In pitched events mode each live cell in a new generation sounds a short tone, its position mapped across span octaves from base. In row as waveform mode the row is read as a single cycle, so the pattern becomes a timbre and every generation changes it. Rule 90 draws Sierpinski's triangle from one live cell; rule 30 never repeats; rule 110 is complicated enough to compute anything.

Worth trying: set rule 90 and move cells to exactly 128, or 64. The pattern annihilates itself and the sound stops. Rule 90 is the exclusive-or of the two neighbours, and on a ring whose length is a power of two every live cell eventually cancels: 64 cells die at generation 32, 128 at generation 64. One cell more or fewer and it runs forever. None of the other rules here care.
