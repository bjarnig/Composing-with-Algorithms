Loops of Different Lengths

The procedure of Brian Eno's Music for Airports (1978). Each note is a tape loop of its own length, and all the loops run at once. Nothing is chosen while the piece plays: the lengths are the only decision, and because they share no common measure the combinations keep changing. Each ring is one loop, and its dot sounds as it passes the top.

a loop's position sounding now

Equal lengths make a chord that returns unchanged. Lengths of 10, 15, 20 and 30 s all fit into one minute, so the whole pattern repeats every minute. Lengths with no common measure take hours or years to come back.
